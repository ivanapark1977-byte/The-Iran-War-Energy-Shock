# The Iran War Energy Shock

**LNG Markets, Price Forecasts, and Project Viability to 2050**

An investigative report by [The Anglophone Blind Spot](https://ivanapark1.substack.com)

---

## About This Report

The 2026 US-Israeli war on Iran has triggered the largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market. This report analyzes the cascading effects on LNG markets, price trajectories through 2050, and the viability of 24 major liquefaction projects in the face of a structural oversupply glut.

### Key Findings

1. **Historic disruption paradox**: The war simultaneously pushed LNG prices to $25.40/MMBtu and destroyed the demand base that incoming capacity was built to serve.

2. **Southeast Asia "skip" effect**: Price-sensitive markets priced out at $21–25/MMBtu are permanently accelerating coal switching and renewable deployment, bypassing LNG as a transition fuel.

3. **Capacity-demand mismatch**: 666 mtpa of capacity arriving by 2028 will create a 255 mtpa surplus, driving JKM to $7.54/MMBtu in 2031 and rendering 9 of 24 major projects unviable.

4. **Qatar's paradox**: Qatar's $3.90/MMBtu breakeven ensures long-run viability, but North Field delays permanently cede market share to US producers with no Hormuz exposure.

## Methodology

**Scenario framework**: Three demand trajectories (Swift Resolution, Prolonged Conflict, Transition Shock) modeled against IEA STEPS baseline.

**Price forecasting**: Four-phase model (War Spike → Crisis Plateau → Oversupply Trough → Long-Run Equilibrium) across JKM, TTF, and Henry Hub benchmarks through 2050.

**Project viability**: All-in breakeven costs (feedgas + liquefaction + shipping + capital recovery) compared against Scenario A price curve at first gas date. Projects sorted by margin (JKM @ first gas minus breakeven).

**Capacity modeling**: Bottom-up project-by-project tracking of sanctioned and unsanctioned capacity additions through 2032, incorporating war-related delays.
